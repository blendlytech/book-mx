## The seven things, ranked

### 1. The ending is not an ending

This is the biggest issue in the manuscript by a wide margin.

Chapter 45 is 485 words and ends on *"Let me explain what happened."* Chapter 46 opens with Glenda's explanation — roughly 500 words of reported exposition — then hands over three deaths, then a cat, then: **"That's it. The end."**

What's wrong with it:

- **The Glenda reversal gets ~900 words.** He walked three days through a desert believing she was in the ground. He grieved her in a wash while men urinated ten feet from his head. That grief is the emotional engine of Chapters 42–43. The reversal is resolved in less space than the mini-fridge gets in Ch38. The reunion needs to be a scene with weight, not a chair and a summary.
- **The crew deaths arrive as an appendix.** Ritchie, Silesh, John-J were built across 7,352 words in Ch9. They die in three paragraphs of reported news, placed *after* the reunion and *before* the cat. June's assessment warned this thread would either need to land or be deferred. It has been neither — it's been dumped. Either it becomes a real movement (Ritchie's unanswered *Happy New Year* is a whole chapter's worth of material) or it becomes four sentences in a coda. Right now it's the largest grief in the book delivered in the smallest available container.
- **Recovery — the second word in "survival and recovery memoir" — is one paragraph.** Omar says six months and offers an English-speaking counselor. Clay signs. Book ends. The reader has watched 120,000 words of a man who cannot stop, and gets no scene of him starting to. The whole ironic architecture — *the building I came to exploit is the one that saved me* — is stated in Ch46 as a thought rather than dramatized. That's the payoff of the entire book and it's currently a sentence.
- **The prologue is never closed.** The Prologue puts you in a chair with a free man touching a scar, saying *the rest of it I'm still trying to explain to myself.* The book never returns to that chair. It has a frame with only one side built.
- **"That's it. The end."** is an author signing off. It's the one moment in 124,000 words where the narrator steps outside the book, and it's the last thing the reader sees.

**What to do:** build a real final movement. Not long — 4,000–6,000 words. The reunion as a scene. The rehab as at least two scenes (the counselor, and one moment where the con voice fails him and something else has to come out). Then return to the chair from the Prologue. End there. Move the crew elegy to its own chapter before the reunion, or cut it to a paragraph in the coda — but don't leave it where it is.

### 2. The craft collapses exactly where the material is hottest

There are two prose registers in this manuscript and a reader will feel the seam.

**Register A** — controlled, unquoted dialogue, sentences that know what they're doing. Ch18, 22, 24, 25, 34, 43, and the Josh chapter are as good as this genre gets.

**Register B** — raw transcription. Typos, doubled spaces, circling repetition, the author talking about the act of writing. And it clusters in the highest-stakes passages in the book:

| Location | What's there |
|---|---|
| `CHAPTER-21.md:13–15` | The David vision — the deepest excavation in the book — told in circling fragments. "absoluetly." "I thought I could handle anything. That was my first mistake. I always thought I could handle anything." |
| `CHAPTER-23.md:31` | A 200-word run-on closing the daughter ceremony, then the orphan line "I'm all in. Not that I really had a choice" — no period. |
| `CHAPTER-36.md:39` | "It took me 20 minutes to write this next line as I couldn't help but break down." The Clayita passage is one of the best things in the book and this line steps in front of it. |
| `CHAPTER-37.md:77–79` | The knockout — the climax of the entire captivity arc — reads like a voice memo. "I cried so hard like a little bitch." "And I chocked that mother fucker out. Die bitch." |
| `CHAPTER-40.md:38` | A stray `Wher` alone on a line, mid-chapter. |
| `CHAPTER-20.md:48` | A stray `J` alone on a line. |
| Scattered | greatful, likeing, evern, wern't, disapeared, crewdly, chocked, mocassins, experences |

Double-space-after-period counts confirm the pattern — the raw-draft chapters are **21 (26), 15 (16), 36 (14), 40 (13), 37 (11), 23 (10)**. Everything else is under 8.

The pattern is legible and human: the closer the material gets to the bone, the more the craft drops away. It's also exactly backwards from what the book needs. Ch37 is the moment the arc turns — six weeks of taking beatings and giving them nothing, and then one day he gives them everything and the mountain changes its mind about him. It should be the most controlled writing in the manuscript. It's currently the least.

Do not sand the profanity out. "Die bitch" can stay if it's *placed* — one line, landed, with silence around it. The problem isn't that it's raw. The problem is that it's unshaped.

### 3. Chapters 10 and 11 are in a different book

Present tense. Staccato fragments. Aphorism per paragraph ("Never trust a town without scars."). Curly apostrophes — 24 in Ch10, 19 in Ch11, essentially zero elsewhere. And **quotation marks on dialogue** at `CHAPTER-10.md:101–103`, which breaks the manuscript's convention everywhere else.

Worse, the compression cost load-bearing material. In June, Ch11 held the father's decline, the million-dollar life insurance policy, the ambulance night, the water-softener goodbye. None of it is in the manuscript now — grep returns zero hits for "life insurance," "policy," "water softener," "ambulance," or any account of the father dying. What survives is 1,364 words of summary.

That matters downstream. Two of the hardest beats in the third act are about the father:

- `CHAPTER-29.md:57` — the itemized deadbeat-son text, closing with *"There. That was worth the money I just sent you."*
- `CHAPTER-33.md:67–71` — the unanswered text from Reina's floor: *"Pass along one word and you might keep your son alive another week."* He never answered.

Both land at maybe 60% of what they should, because the reader has 1,300 words of that relationship instead of 6,000. Whatever else gets cut, **the father has to be a person before he can be a closed door.**

### 4. Four orphan chapters, and where three of them go

Ginger, Jimmy, Josh, Two Worlds — 7,805 words, all four strong, none placed.

You already built the mechanism for three of them. Ch22 (Carrie) and Ch25 (Eleanor) are portraits triggered by an ayahuasca vision — the medicine opens a door, the narrative steps through into a portrait, then returns to the mat. That template works and it's sitting there unused.

- **Two Worlds** and **Jimmy** belong in or beside that Sinaloa cluster. They're the same excavation — who I was made into. And Two Worlds contains the single best thesis sentence in the manuscript: *"Alcohol was the only thing I ever found that made me one person."* That line is currently in a file with no home. It should be load-bearing.
- **Ginger** goes late in the Michigan/Ohio decline (10–11), because its climax — sitting in a parked car twenty yards from the woman who raised him, too ashamed to get out — is a scene about being skinny, and that's exactly where he's skinny.
- **Josh is not a portrait. It's a plot revelation, and it is out of the book.** It reveals that Omar was inside Clay's accounts sending threats to Josh's wife and children *in the same hours* Clay was telling him the truth. That fact retroactively explains the money in Ch40 and it is the cleanest statement of what the whole book is actually about:

  > *"You spend the currency down to nothing, and then one day something true happens to you — the truest thing that will ever happen to you — and you go to spend it and there's not one cent left in the account."*

  That's your thesis. That belongs around Ch40–41, and it may belong on the back cover instead of what's there now.

### 5. Zaul's reversal is under-played

`CHAPTER-44.md:61–87`. Clay, in detox, sees Zaul alive — a patient in the building. This is the structural full-circle the entire book pivots on. The man whose disappearance caused every event of the last 40,000 words, alive, on the wrong side of the cage, in the rehab Clay flew down to exploit.

It gets about twenty lines. The joke is perfect — *"we can still get that call center back up and running"* — and the scene around it is thin. Second-largest reversal in the book, shorter than the description of a mini-fridge.

### 6. Repetition that survived, and one motif that's become a tic

- **The dial / the switch.** Ch5, 10, 11, 13, 17 — fine, that's a motif being built. Then Ch27 (×2), Ch28 (×3), Ch29 (×2), Ch31. **Eight uses in five chapters.** In the collapse stretch it's stopped being a figure and become a stage direction. Cut it to one appearance in that whole run, and make it the one in Ch28 where it fuses with Brewmetrics — *"My sobriety was vibe-coded"* — which is the best version of the idea in the book.
- **The geographic cure** is still at 8 chapters. Same note as June: it wants half.
- **The crying.** The withheld-tears thread pays off perfectly at `CHAPTER-35.md:7` — a man who gave them nothing through a boot, a beating, and a gun barrel breaks the second her body is against his. That is the payoff. Then he cries in 36, 37, 40 (twice), 43, and 45. Seven total. And at `CHAPTER-45.md:13` the narrator flags it himself: *"I cried like a baby — again, the second time in a month."* When the prose starts noticing its own repetition, that's the tell. Ch35, Ch40 (the cat), and Ch43 (the wash) are the three that earn it. The others should be described without the word.

### 7. Smaller snags

- **`CHAPTER-37.md:3`** — "Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's have all passed by without an honorable mention by me." That's a shrug at the reader about missing time. Five months in a black-painted bus and there is no Christmas on that mountain. That isn't an omission to apologize for; it's a scene you haven't written.
- **Brandy contact order.** Ch42 has him reaching Brandy from Reina's floor and being told no. `CHAPTER-46.md:17` says "The only person I got through to was Brandy, and that was a week ago, when they gave me back my laptop." On a fast read those contradict. One clarifying clause fixes it.
- **`CHAPTER-46.md:79`** — "A broken compass. It's not like a clock. It isn't right twice a day." Good line, wrong position. It's currently the second-to-last thing before "That's it. The end." In a rebuilt ending it could be the last line of the book.

## What to protect

Short list, because it tells you where not to point the red pen.

**Ch18** (the drive into Sinaloa) is the model for the whole book — dread built entirely out of event and silence, interpretation withheld. **Ch22** (Carrie) and **Ch25** (Eleanor) are the two best-written chapters in the manuscript; Eleanor's "They lost theirs. We kept ours" reveal — that the arithmetic running under his whole life was handed to him by a woman trying to say she loved him — is the finest single move in the book. **Ch34** (the beating) is restrained in exactly the way that material has to be restrained. **Ch43** (the wash) earns its epiphany because it's physical, not stated. **Josh** is a top-five chapter sitting outside the book.

And **Ch26, "The Cruel Joke,"** is your actual thesis:

> *"The grief was never what the drink was standing on. The drink stands on nothing but itself."*

That's an uncommon and unsentimental claim for a recovery memoir, and you spent 15,000 words of ayahuasca earning the right to make it. It should be more visible in the pitch than it currently is.

## Suggested order of work

1. **Build the ending.** 4,000–6,000 words. Reunion as scene, rehab as scene, return to the Prologue's chair. Relocate or compress the crew elegy. This is the only thing standing between you and a finished book.
2. **Line-pass the six raw chapters** — 21, 23, 36, 37, 40, and 15 — to Register A. Keep every ugly true thing; shape it.
3. **Restore the father.** Rebuild Ch10–11 in past tense, in the book's voice, with enough of the relationship that Ch29 and Ch33 land at full weight.
4. **Place the orphans.** Josh late, Ginger in the decline, Jimmy and Two Worlds in or beside the Sinaloa cluster.
5. **Expand Zaul.** Give the reversal in Ch44 twice the room.
6. **Motif pass.** Dial/switch down to one in Ch27–31; geographic cure halved; crying down to three.
7. **Then cut for length.** After all of the above, target 95–105k. The fat is no longer in the flashback — it's in Ch27–31 (the collapse re-runs the "good thing dies" beat three times: Zaul, Brewmetrics, San Diego) and in the middle of the ayahuasca cluster.
