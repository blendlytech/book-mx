# Chapter 51 — Where the Ink Runs Out
This is where the ink runs out.

Not because the story is clean, and not because I went and fixed every broken thing I left behind me. If you’ve read this far, you know better than that. I’m forty-seven years old, standing on the edge of my own life with a trail of wreckage stretching from Ohio down to Sinaloa.

I’m stopping because the manuscript has to end somewhere, and if I keep writing, I’m just going to start pitching again.

That’s the final trap. A man like me gets to the end of his own reckoning and immediately starts looking for a neat bow to tie around it. He wants to sell you on the turn-around. He wants to draft an epilogue that turns a lifetime of running into some noble, heroic journey of self-discovery.

It wasn’t a journey. It was just damage, and ninety percent of it was hand-delivered by me.

So there’s no grand wrap-up. No neat resolution. No soft music playing as the sun sets over Mexicali. 

Everything I own, everything I’ve done, and every piece of truth I could drag out of myself is sitting right here on these pages. I didn’t dress it up, and I didn’t hide the ugly parts to save my own skin. 

If my daughter ever finds this twenty years from now, she won't have to guess who her father was. She’ll have the unvarnished truth. If Zach reads it, he knows I’ll go to bat for him until the day I die. If Niki reads it, she knows I carry every kindness she ever gave me, even the ones I didn't deserve.

The machine is off. The story is told. 

I’m closing the laptop, setting down the pen, and shutting the door on the past for good. 

That’s it. That’s the end of the book.
