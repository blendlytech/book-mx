## Main blurb

*"This is how you ruin your life by forty-five. Pay attention."*

Clay Mills got good at exactly one thing: running. A Las Vegas phone man who sold strangers luxury vacations to their own hometowns and drank through every dollar he conned, he outran the wreckage of his life one state line at a time — Vegas, Alaska, a jail cell, a barn in Michigan — always dead sure the next town would be the one that finally fixed him.

It never was.

Then a woman he'd loved for three years through a phone screen, and never once touched, got sober and changed the math. Chasing her, Clay crossed illegally into Mexico on a one-way ticket, hunting one more fresh start — and found a cartel mountain two hours outside Rosarito, where the men who owned the ground turned a dog loose on him for sport, and where, for a long stretch, dying would have been the kinder draw.

This is the raw, unsparing true story of an addict's "geographic cure" chased all the way to the end of the map: the scams, the love, the relapses, the captivity, and the brutal road back. No moralizing. No flinching. Just a man telling you exactly how he ended up facedown in the dirt of a country he had no right to be in — and exactly how he walked back out.

*Because you're going to want to know how a man ends up where he ended up. And the only honest answer is: one bad left turn at a time.*

## Short version (for tighter cover layouts, ~70 words)

A Las Vegas con man and lifelong addict chases a woman he's never touched across an illegal border — and runs his "geographic cure" straight up a cartel mountain outside Rosarito, into captivity, and to the very edge of his own life. Unvarnished, brutal, and grimly funny, this is the true story of how a man ends up facedown in the dirt of a foreign country, and how he found his way back. One bad left turn at a time.
