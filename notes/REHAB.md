# Rehab and Backstory

Glenda had already been in rehab for 3 months at this time, and I had been sober for 2 weeks. We both had struggled with alcohol our entire lives and both liked to smoke weed.  We had both dabbled in cocaine, but it was never really a persistent problem or addiction.  Our drugs of choice were alcohol and weed.  

I had connected the director of the rehab, Zaul, with the Solar company I was selling solar for at the time - to create a call center within the rehab setting appointments for solar sales closers in the US.  I had many conversations with him over video chat over the past 2 months through Glenda's phone.  Glenda had special privledges as a long time repeat resident. This was her third tour.  She was on the "other side" of the cage, which meant she could come and go with permission, but was checked and tested at the door for alchohol and drugs every time she returned and she had a curfew. I was to receive a percentage of the revenue generated from the call center and Glenda and I were to commit to staying sober together as I would also be tested when I entered the center daily to set this all up and train the English speaking residents to take appointments.  She had 3 more months left of rehab and I was to setup the call center, train and manage it until I could train my replacement, while still working for the same solar company in sales until the call center money started rolling in.  I'd live in a nearby AirBnB and wait until Glenda fullfilled her requried commitment.  It was perfect.  Only one problem... turns out no matter how much you tell yourself you have your addiction licked, it's always lying in wait for the perfect opportunity to strike you down.  

Getting sober isn't easy, and staying sober is even harder.  It's a constant battle, a constant struggle.  It's a daily commitment, a daily choice.  It's a marathon, not a sprint.  And sometimes, even if you're running the race of your life, you can still fall.  You can still stumble.  You can still lose.  But that doesn't mean you give up.  That doesn't mean you quit.  That doesn't mean you stop fighting.  It just means you get back up, and you keep going.  One step at a time.  One day at a time.  One breath at a time.  Until you're finally free.
